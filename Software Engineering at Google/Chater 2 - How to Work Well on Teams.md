# Chapter 2 - How to Work Well on Teams

### The Genius Myth

Myth: individuals are idolized for their achievements
Reality: most successful projects involve teamwork

- Overemphasis on individual genius: Programmers feel pressure to work alone and hide their mistakes, fearing that others will judge them.
- Fear of sharing ideas: Developers hesitate to share their work-in-progress, worrying that others might steal their idea or criticize their code.
- Insecurity and perfectionism: The need for secrecy leads to increased stress and a desire to polish code until it's perfect before sharing.

Better to do these:

- Share work-in-progress: Collaborate with others, share ideas, and get feedback early on.
- Emphasize teamwork: Recognize that most projects require collaboration and diverse skills.
- Focus on solving human problems: Prioritize creating software that solves real-world problems rather than just showcasing individual genius.

These habits lead to:

- healthier attitudes towards collaboration
- better software development outcomes

### Hiding Considered Harmful

- Bus Factor: The number of people who need to be hit by a bus before a project is completely doomed. Working with others increases the bus factor, making it less likely that a single person's absence will derail the project.
- Early Sharing: Sharing work-in-progress and ideas early on can help prevent personal missteps and strengthen the team's collective wisdom.
- Feedback Loops: Tight feedback loops are essential for successful software development. This means working in small iterations, testing frequently, and refining code continuously.
- Team Collaboration: Working with others directly increases the collective wisdom behind a project. Peers can provide instant feedback and help solve problems more efficiently.
- Shifting Left: The DevOps philosophy emphasizes getting feedback as early as possible, testing as early as possible, and thinking about security and production environments as early as possible.

### The Three Pillars of Social Interaction

1. Humility
2. Respect
3. Trust

- do not underestimate the power of playing the social game
- it’s about creating relationships
- relationships always outlast projects

### Blameless Post-Mortem Culture

- Postmortems: Conduct thorough postmortem analyses of failures to learn from mistakes and prevent them from happening again.
- Collaboration styles: Different people have different working styles, and it's essential to find ways to collaborate effectively despite these differences.
- Patience: Be patient with others, especially when working on challenging projects, and be willing to adapt your approach as needed.
- Influence and vulnerability: Being open to influence from others and willing to show vulnerability can actually increase your credibility and respect within a team.
